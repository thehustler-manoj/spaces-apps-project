The Moon · Apollo sites

Footprints that never fade

Between 1969 and 1972, Apollo astronauts landed on the Moon and came home. They left a lot behind: the lower halves of their landing craft, scientific instruments, tools, and on three missions, a Lunar Roving Vehicle.

With no wind or rain on the Moon, these things stay almost exactly as they were left. Bootprints can last for a very long time.

Nothing has moved. Nobody has touched them. They are waiting.
Mars · Spirit and Opportunity

Two rovers, one long goodbye

Spirit and Opportunity landed on Mars in 2004 and were built to work for about 90 days. They kept going for years. Spirit got stuck in soft sand and went silent in 2010. Opportunity was caught in a huge dust storm in 2018 and never woke up; its mission was declared over in 2019.

They still sit on Mars, covered by red dust, having sent back a huge amount of science and photographs.

Mars · Ingenuity

The little helicopter that flew

Ingenuity was supposed to make a few test flights. It flew dozens of times and became the first aircraft to fly on another planet. After its rotor blades were damaged, its final flight was in January 2024. It stays where it landed.

Why it matters

Abandoned, but never forgotten

These machines are more than leftover equipment. They are proof of what people can build and dare to try. Each one is a small piece of human history sitting on another world.

One day, someone may go back to visit them.
